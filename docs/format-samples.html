Format A · self-created
Format A

The Take Card

The whole post is typography you set. There is no third-party asset to clear, no rectangle to hatch, and nothing that expires. This is the format that should carry roughly 40% of the feed, and it is the one people skip because it feels like less work than it is.

What makes it work: the line has to be strong enough to survive without a picture propping it up. That is a real constraint and it is also the point — it forces the writing to do the job the borrowed still used to do. The eyebrow and date give it recurring-column authority; the rule and wordmark make it recognizable at thumbnail size in a grid.

Format B · licensed base + original commentary
Format B

Voiceover Commentary

The clip is someone else's; the argument over the top is yours. The waveform is the load-bearing detail — it runs edge to edge, unbroken, because your voice is present for the whole duration rather than bookending a silent replay.

What makes it work: burned-in captions from your own voiceover, not auto-captions of the source. Look at the difference that makes — the on-screen words are your words directing attention at a specific thing in the frame. That is the Hosseinzadeh posture rendered visually, and it is also what Instagram's originality check is scanning for. A silent stretch in that waveform is the tell that you have slipped back into reposting.

Format C · native collab, authorship shared
Format C

Native Collab & Repost

Two avatars in the header instead of one, and both handles on the byline. That header is the entire legal argument: authorship is shared rather than transferred, so no reproduction happens and there is no mark of yours sitting where theirs should be.

What makes it work: it is a trade, not a taking. The original creator gets your reach, you get the post and your caption sits on it. Compare that to the audited account's house-branded card — same content, but one version makes a copy and swaps the attribution, and the other does neither. This should carry about 25% of the feed and it costs nothing.

Format D · express grant, credit preserved
Format D

The Permissioned Clip

The green dot and the handle sitting in the corner of the frame are not decoration. That is the creator's mark, preserved in the position they put it — the single visual difference between this and a § 1202 claim.

What makes it work: the credit appears twice, in the frame and in the caption, and the caption credits before it comments. That ordering matters more than it looks: it signals to the creator community that permission is how you operate, which is what makes the next twenty asks land. One DM converts an infringement into a licence — this format is the highest-leverage habit in the whole system.

Format E · owned, registrable, licensable
Format E

Owned Footage

Rendered completely again — because like Format A, there is nothing in it that belongs to anyone else. The abstraction here is mine as an illustrator, not a rights placeholder.

What makes it work: the copyright bug in the corner is doing something the other four formats cannot. Every other format on this page is about not being sued. This one is about being the party who could sue — register it and the exact statutory-damages machinery that threatens an aggregator starts working for you instead. It is also the only content a competitor structurally cannot take, and the reason press credentials are worth chasing at your size.

The kit that makes Format A repeatable

Formats A and E are the ones you fully own, and A is the one you will post three times a day. That only works if the card is a template rather than a design decision each time. Four things fix it in place:

Palette — three values

Ground, type, and one accent that only ever appears as a rule, an eyebrow, or a single emphasized word. Three is enough; a fourth invites a decision.

Type — two roles
Display · serif · 600
1.42rem @ 1.16 line-height
−0.012em tracking
——
Utility · mono · 0.58rem
0.16em tracking · uppercase

One face carries the take, one carries the furniture. No third face, ever.

Anatomy — fixed slots

Eyebrow — column name, left. Date — right. The line — bottom-anchored so length varies upward and the baseline never moves. Rule — 2px accent, 2.5rem. Wordmark — last.

The grid test

Shrink the card to 120px. If the eyebrow colour and the rule still identify it as yours with the words illegible, the template works. If it reads as a generic quote card, it does not.

Mockups, not screenshots.
