Exactly What Gets Sent

Every block used to generate Rufus, in the order it is assembled, with the locked parts marked. Copy them verbatim — editing a locked block is how a character drifts.

How a prompt is built

A prompt is Subject + Scene + Camera + Negative, concatenated in that order. Only varies blocks change between posts. Everything marked locked is copied character-for-character every single time.

01

Images — both identities

Identity is carried by lighting and situation, not by the subject block. The subject is identical in both modes.

Rogue revised — three rules changed

1. Rogue is no longer nocturnal and solitary. Night-and-alone is atmospheric but isolating, and isolation kills both relatability and the joke. Daytime with witnesses is funnier and far more shareable. The mode is now distinguished by behaviour, the crushed pouch, and how people react — not by darkness.

2. One accessory is allowed. This reverses the previous no-accessories rule, and the reference evidence is decisive: a photoreal pigeon in a NY cap and boots ran 139K likes against 112K shares. The distinction that matters is styling versus costume. A cap sitting slightly crooked reads as attitude; a hi-vis vest and lanyard reads as a mascot and kills the realism. One culturally loaded item, never an outfit.

3. Density and disorder are ingredients, not background. The strongest reference is a horse in a home studio so packed with cables and gear that the animal is buried in human mess. Empty rooms read as staged; cluttered ones read as real.

Brand-safety carve-out: the pigeon reference also has a lit cigarette. Do not copy that. Tobacco imagery draws restriction and demonetisation on both Meta and TikTok, and it buys nothing the cap does not.

Trademark carve-out — the cap must stay illegible. “Navy baseball cap” is close to a synonym for one team’s cap in the training data: across a five-frame test the interlocking NY mark rendered in three of them, once on a bystander never described as wearing a cap at all. Negatives do not suppress it. A famous mark on the protagonist is not incidental depiction — it recurs every post and functions as part of the character, which is the use Jack Daniel’s v. VIP Products (2023) put outside the expressive-works shelter, and Rufus’s storyline of intoxication and theft is a tarnishment fact pattern. The canon is now a worn-out, unreadable emblem. Real-world brand texture belongs in the environment — shelf packaging, background extras — where depiction genuinely is incidental. The acceptance test is one question: could a viewer name the team? If yes, reject the frame or clean it with delogo.py.

Gate the pouch — it drifts, and it drifts silently

The pouch is the mode indicator, so a drifted pouch is a broken post. It fails in three ways and all three have now been observed: colour going magenta instead of purple, band orientation going vertical or diagonal, and object type rendering as a snack bag instead of a foil drink pouch. The first wrecked-store generation failed on all three at once — body sampled at RGB (174, 51, 107) against a target of (150, 110, 205).

Fix it with an object-level pass, do not regenerate the scene. When the composition is good, replacing the pouch takes one edit and preserves everything else. The corrected frame sampled (180, 139, 200) — inside tolerance.

And check the last frame of every video, not just the source. Colour degrades during generation: over six seconds the pouch shifted from (180, 139, 200) to (141, 65, 141) — still purple, still passing, but visibly travelling toward magenta. A longer clip would fail.

docs/check-pouch.py automates all of this: it finds the yellow band, measures body colour against target, tests band orientation, and exits non-zero on failure so it can gate a batch.

Post-production order — the label is embedded, never overlaid

The flat composite (solid yellow rectangle + straight text) was retired: it deleted the band’s wrinkles and highlights, so the label read as a sticker floating over the photo. The pipeline that replaced it, in order, per frame:

1. Generate the pouch blank — purple body, level yellow band, no text. Models render gibberish type; don’t ask for the wordmark.
2. lock-sippy.py pins body and band to canon hue and saturation while leaving per-pixel value alone, so scene lighting survives. Pass --box in any cluttered frame — auto-detect has locked onto crisp packets. The body finder unions the regions above and below the band; excluding whole band rows once cut the pouch in half and left a colour seam.
3. embed-sippy.py sets the type into the surface: it traces the band’s own top and bottom edges per column, warps the baseline through that curve so the letters bend where the bag slumps, and lights the ink with the band’s luminance — shadows and speculars cross the letters. Tighten --span off a steep dip or the end glyphs shear.
4. check-frame.py gates the result. It tests hue, not distance-to-swatch (distance conflates drift with dim light), and band tilt via fitted centreline slope, not bounding box (a sagging band is realism, a rotated band is a defect).

The through-line, and why it maximises shares

All three references put the animal inside a dense human world with humans visibly reacting to it — or, in the pigeon's case, wearing the human world. That is what converts a curiosity into something a viewer sends to someone. Shares need a surrogate in frame: a person having the reaction the viewer would have. An empty night street gives them nobody to be.

02

Video — the motion revision

Spec change: more motion

The original Living Still asked for near-zero movement — roughly 1.2% change between frames. That reads as deadpan in isolation but is too static to hold a feed, where movement is what buys watch time. Videos now brief for continuous, visible action.

The constraint that does not move: more motion must never mean human motion. Every added movement has to be behaviour a real raccoon performs. The moment Rufus points, shrugs, waves or gestures, the photorealism dies — and the photorealism is the entire hook. The impossibility stays in the situation, never in the body.

Motion vocabulary — build shots from these

MovementWhy it works
Rapid dexterous paw workRaccoons genuinely manipulate objects at speed. The single most watchable thing they do, and it is real
Sharp head swivels, glancing off-cameraReads as checking whether anyone is coming — motion that also tells story
Ear flicks and rotationConstant micro-movement that keeps a frame alive between beats
Rising higher on the hind legsWeight shift, a real posture change, adds vertical dynamics
Sniffing, nose workingSpecies-typical, cheap, always in character
Tail sweepLarge slow movement that balances the fast paw work
Startle then freezeThe best closer: action, then dead stillness locked on the lens
Drop to all fours and back upBig movement, entirely natural, resets the frame
Video prompt — structureLocked shape
The raccoon is ACTIVELY BUSY and moving throughout the shot. Continuous
motion, never static.

[ACTION — two or three beats from the motion vocabulary, in order, ending
with: then it snaps its head back, locks eyes directly into the camera lens
and freezes for the last beat, caught.]

All movement must be REAL RACCOON MOVEMENT: scurrying, fidgeting, sniffing,
head-jerks, ear twitches, weight shifts on the hind legs, dexterous paw
manipulation. It must NEVER move like a human — no human gestures, no
pointing, no shrugging, no waving, no anthropomorphic body language.

Handheld phone camera, natural shake, following the action slightly.

AUDIO: [diegetic sources for this scene]. ABSOLUTELY NO MUSIC. NO SPEECH,
NO VOICES, NO NARRATION, NO HUMAN TALKING, no dialogue of any kind.

Photorealistic, preserving the lighting, colour cast, sensor noise, grain
and soft focus of the source image. Real handheld phone video, not a
rendered animation. No stylisation.
Measured against the old spec

Same start frame, both models. Mean change between frames went from 1.18% to 2.91% — roughly two and a half times the movement — with peaks up from 3.1% to 5.9%. He genuinely works the keypad, turns into the machine, glances back, and reaches for the notes. The movement reads as raccoon: no human gestures appeared, and the crushed pouch stayed in frame throughout.

What did not land: the closing beat. The brief asked him to snap back and freeze on the lens for the final second; instead the model distributed the eye contact into the middle of the clip and ended him facing the machine. If the lock-on matters for a given post, put it first rather than last, or plan to trim the tail.

Settings

flux_3_video, start_image = the approved still, generate_audio: true, 3:4, 1080p, 5–6 seconds. Decline the model's cinematic preset suggestion — it adds camera moves the format does not want. And with real motion in the shot, do not ping-pong: reversed action is obvious, and reversed audio doubly so. Cut a clean end instead of looping.
