Written for the actual situation: **starting from zero followers**, **8–12

## 1 · The strategy on one page

You are not going to reach $1,000/month by accumulating view payouts. The
arithmetic forbids it: at the ~$0.50 RPM that entertainment content earns on
TikTok, $1,000 means **two million qualified views every month** — and only
from videos over 60 seconds, which is a fraction of your output.

The path that actually works is a ladder, and each rung unlocks the next.

| Rung | What it is | Why it comes here |
|---|---|---|
| **1. TikTok cold start** | Grind TikTok to 10K, then 100K | The only platform that gives a zero-follower account real distribution. Everything else needs an audience you don't have yet. |
| **2. Cross-post from day one** | Same assets to IG, FB, YouTube Shorts | Costs nothing (the pipeline already does it) and builds the follower counts the later rungs check. |
| **3. TikTok Creator Rewards** | 10K followers + 100K views/30d | First real money. Small — but it's the proof the account works. |
| **4. Facebook Creator Fast Track** | **Guaranteed $1,000/mo for 3 months** | This is your $1,000. It gates on follower count *elsewhere* — which rungs 1–2 built. |
| **5. Brand deals + BIG SIPPY merch** | The durable business | Platform money is rent. This is equity. |

**The single most important finding in this document:** Facebook's Creator
Fast Track, launched March 2026, pays established creators a **guaranteed
$1,000/month for three months** to post Reels on Facebook. It requires 15
Reels per 30 days across at least 10 different days — *your pipeline already
produces double that*. Content does **not** need to be Facebook-exclusive, so
it's the same shorts you're already posting. And critically, **AI-generated
content is explicitly permitted** as long as it's original to you.

The entry gate is roughly **100K followers on Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube**
($3,000/mo at 1M+), with some reported entry near 20K given strong
engagement. It also grants immediate access to Facebook Content Monetization,
which keeps paying after the three guaranteed months end.

So the whole strategy compresses to: **get to 100K on TikTok, then collect.**

## 2 · Platform deep dives

### 2.1 · TikTok — the engine

**What it actually is for you:** the only one of the four where a brand-new
account with zero followers can put a video in front of a million people. Its
recommendation system is content-first, not graph-first — it tests every
upload on a small audience and escalates based on performance. That property
is why TikTok is rung one and everything else is rung two.

**How it ranks.** The dominant signal is **completion rate**, followed by
**rewatch**. A 7-second video watched twice by 60% of viewers outperforms a
45-second video watched halfway. This is the single mechanical reason the
post-format plan puts Living Stills (5–7s loops) at ~40% of output — they are
engineered to be completed and rewatched.

After completion: shares, comments, likes, in that order. Follows-from-video
and profile-visits-from-video are strong secondary signals that specifically
tell TikTok "this creator is worth surfacing again."

**Where Rufus fits.** Photoreal-animal-doing-human-things is a proven TikTok
category with a critical property: it is **legible in under a second**. No
setup, no context, no narration needed. The viewer sees a raccoon in a cap
behind a register and understands the entire joke instantly. That's a
completion-rate machine.

**Posting windows (US audience).** The engagement-velocity mechanic matters
more than the clock: TikTok tests a new post on a small audience and the
**first 30–60 minutes decide whether it escalates**. So the goal is posting
when *your* followers are most likely to be awake and scrolling, not when
"TikTok is busiest" globally.

- Primary: **6–9pm ET** weekdays (the reliable scroll window)
- Secondary: **11am–1pm ET** (lunch)
- Third slot if posting 3×: **7–9am ET**

For the first 90 days, **consistency beats optimization**. Post at the same
times daily. The system learns your schedule and pre-positions your content;
erratic timing costs you more than a suboptimal hour does.

**Ceiling before problems.** TikTok tolerates high volume — 3–4 posts/day is
normal for a brand account. The real risk isn't a rate limit, it's
**self-cannibalization** (your posts competing for the same audience slot)
and the **unoriginal-content classifier**. Keep posts ≥3 hours apart. Never
exceed 4/day.

**Branding setup.** Handle identical across all four platforms. Bio in
Rufus's voice, not a description of the account ("community liaison.
currently between roles." beats "Daily AI raccoon comedy"). Pin three videos:
the pivot (mode-flip explainer), the best Turn, and the strongest chaos
piece — this is the lore starter kit that converts a profile visit into a
follow.

**Comments.** TikTok comments are a ranking signal *and* a content surface —
comment replies can be posted as videos, which is free content that the
algorithm treats as new. Reply in character, always. Your own pinned comment
under a chaotic post ("he is doing his best") reliably becomes top comment
and doubles comment velocity.

### 2.2 · Instagram — the audience, not the income

**What it actually is for you:** the place where the follower count that
unlocks Facebook Fast Track and brand deals gets *counted*, and where brands
look you up before they email you. It is a portfolio and a credential.

**It does not pay for views.** This is the most important correction in this
document. The Reels Play Bonus was discontinued in 2023. What remains are
invite-only, seasonal bonuses — the Breakthrough Bonus offers up to
$1,000/month at 100K+ followers — that Instagram itself describes as limited
time, doesn't publish rates for, and which no one can plan around. **Budget
zero Instagram view revenue.**

**How it ranks.** Instagram's top distribution signal for Reels is
**sends per reach** — how often a viewer DMs your post to someone else. Not
likes. Not even shares-to-story. Direct sends. After that: watch time,
saves, then likes (weakest).

**Saves are the sleeper signal.** They tell Instagram the content has
reference value. Carousels and stills get saved far more than video — which
is why the Two-Mode Carousel is Instagram-only in the format plan.

**Where Rufus fits.** "Send this to someone" is the exact behaviour deadpan
absurdist content produces — people DM it with "this is you" attached. That
is Instagram's #1 signal, and this content is naturally optimized for it.
The captions should exploit this: understatement invites a viewer to supply
the reaction, and supplying the reaction usually means sending it to a
friend.

**Posting windows.** 11am–1pm ET and 7–9pm ET. Instagram's audience is more
schedule-bound than TikTok's. Stories 2–3×/day spread across waking hours —
stories are the retention instrument, not a reach instrument.

**Ceiling.** 1–2 feed posts/day plus Reels. Instagram is materially less
tolerant of volume than TikTok; 3+ feed posts/day reads as spam behaviour
and depresses reach on all of them. Stories are effectively uncapped.

**Format notes.** Feed = curation (your best work, the grid is a portfolio
brands will judge). Reels = reach. Carousels = saves, IG-only, never
cross-posted. **The 60s episode never goes here** — Reels ranks on
completion, and a 60s episode loses to its own 8-second excerpt.

**Instagram's real job:** be good enough that a brand's marketing coordinator
sees a professional, consistent, on-brand account when they check you out.

### 2.3 · Facebook — the surprise winner

**What it actually is for you:** the highest per-view payer of the four, and
the home of the single program most likely to deliver your first $1,000.

**Everyone underrates it and that is the opportunity.** Facebook Content
Monetization (which merged in-stream ads, Reels ads, and the Performance
Bonus into one program) pays roughly **$0.15–$4 RPM** depending on length,
niche, and engagement. Compare: TikTok $0.40–1.00, YouTube Shorts $0.01–0.10.
Facebook's ceiling is several times TikTok's and orders above Shorts.

**Creator Fast Track is the headline.** Guaranteed **$1,000/month for three
months** ($3,000 at 1M+ followers elsewhere), requiring:
- Professional Mode toggled on
- **15 Reels per 30 days, posted across at least 10 different days**
- Content original to you — **AI-generated content explicitly allowed**
- Not required to be Facebook-exclusive
- An existing audience on IG / TikTok / YouTube (~100K for the top tier;
  reports of entry around 20K with strong engagement)

Your pipeline produces roughly 30 shorts/month. The posting requirement is
already met twice over. **The only missing input is follower count elsewhere
— which is what rungs 1–2 exist to build.**

**Demographic caveat, stated honestly.** Facebook skews older than your 16–34
target. That is a real mismatch — but animal content is one of the few
categories that travels across every demographic, and the Fast Track money
does not care whether the viewer is 22 or 52. Treat Facebook as a monetization
surface first and an audience-building surface second.

**How it ranks.** Shares and dwell time dominate. Facebook's audience shares
publicly (to their own feed) far more than Instagram's, which suits
spectacle content — Rogue Rufus outperforms Community Rufus here.

**Posting windows.** 8–10am ET and 6–9pm ET. Facebook's audience is
older and more likely to check in the morning than TikTok's.

**Ceiling.** 3–5 Reels/day tolerated on a Page. Facebook is the most
volume-tolerant of the four. Keep to the pipeline's natural output.

**Setup requirement:** a Facebook **Page** (not a personal profile) with
Professional Mode on. The Page is already a prerequisite for the Instagram
Business account the pipeline publishes through, so this costs nothing extra.

### 2.4 · YouTube Shorts — the long game

*(Note: you said "YouTube Reels" — the product is **YouTube Shorts**. Reels
is Meta's brand. Same 9:16 format.)*

**What it actually is for you:** the worst short-term payer and the best
long-term asset. Two reasons to be there despite terrible Shorts RPM.

**The RPM is genuinely bad.** $0.01–$0.10 per 1,000 views, because Shorts
revenue is a shared pool split across all eligible Shorts views and creators
take 45% of the allocation — not a direct ad placement. At $0.05 RPM,
$1,000/month would need **20 million monthly views**. Ignore Shorts revenue
as a income line.

**Why be there anyway:**
1. **Search permanence.** YouTube is a search engine. A TikTok is dead in 72
   hours; a YouTube video accrues views for years. "Raccoon" searches will
   find Rufus in 2028.
2. **The 60s episode has a real home here.** Shorts allows up to 3 minutes,
   and your weekly episode is genuinely suited to it — where on Instagram it
   would actively underperform.
3. **YPP unlocks more than Shorts ads:** channel memberships, Super Thanks,
   and YouTube Shopping — which is where BIG SIPPY merch integrates natively.

**YPP thresholds:** 1,000 subscribers **plus either** 10 million Shorts views
in 90 days **or** 4,000 long-form watch hours. The Shorts path is brutal; the
watch-hours path is why the weekly episode matters — longer content
accumulates watch hours far faster than 8-second loops.

**Posting windows.** 12–3pm ET and 7–10pm ET, but YouTube is the least
timing-sensitive of the four because search and suggested traffic dominate
after the first 48 hours.

**Ceiling.** 1–3 Shorts/day. YouTube penalizes low-retention uploads more
than the others — a weak Short depresses the channel, not just the video.
**Post your best work here, not everything.** This is the one platform where
you should filter rather than firehose.

**⚠ The "Made for Kids" trap.** Animal content frequently gets classified as
made-for-kids, which **disables personalized ads (collapsing RPM), comments,
and notifications**. Rufus's deadpan-crime framing is adult-oriented — mark
the channel and every video **Not Made for Kids**, and keep the branding
unmistakably adult in tone. This is a real risk that has killed otherwise
healthy animal channels.

## 4 · The two characters as engagement instruments

Community Rufus and Rogue Rufus are not just tonal variety — they produce
**mechanically different engagement**, and that should drive where each one
leads.

| | **Rufus the Raccoon** (community) | **Rufus Gone Rogue** |
|---|---|---|
| Emotional register | Recognition — "this is my coworker" | Spectacle — "what is happening" |
| Dominant signal produced | **Saves and comments** | **Shares and stitches** |
| Why | Relatable competence-failure is a *reference* — people save it and tag colleagues | Chaos is a *social object* — people broadcast it |
| Best platform | **Instagram** (saves + DM sends are IG's top signals) | **Facebook + TikTok** (public sharing, spectacle) |
| Caption type that fits | Type A in-scene irony ("new hire") | Type B unrelated biology (maximum mismatch against chaos) |
| Risk | Too mild alone — no spectacle, slow growth | Too much alone — no relatability, viewers don't see themselves |

**The ratio: 60/40 community-forward early.** The rogue lands harder when
the baseline is established — you cannot subvert a norm you haven't
established yet. Once the audience knows Community Rufus, every Rogue post
is a payoff rather than random noise.

**The Turn is the highest-value format** precisely because it contains both
in one asset: the recognition hook holds the first three seconds, the flip
produces the share. That's why it's ~25% of the mix and why the weekly
episode is structured as an extended Turn.

**Never split them across accounts.** The contrast *is* the product.

## 5 · Flagging, ceilings, and platform risk

### Practical posting ceilings

| Platform | Comfortable | Hard ceiling | What actually triggers trouble |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 2–3/day | 4/day | Self-cannibalization and the unoriginal-content classifier, not a rate limit. Space posts ≥3 hours. |
| Instagram | 1–2 feed + Reels | 3 feed/day | Volume reads as spam and depresses reach across *all* your posts |
| Facebook | 3–4 Reels/day | 5/day | Most volume-tolerant of the four |
| YouTube Shorts | 1–2/day | 3/day | Low-retention uploads depress the whole channel — filter here |

### The AI-content reality

**Label AI content on Meta surfaces — it is required.** But understand what
the actual risk is: the label itself is *not* a ranking penalty.
**Mass-produced repetition is.** Ten posts that look like the same scene with
one prop swapped will be suppressed regardless of labeling.

This is why the rotation calendar bans scene reuse within 14 days, and why
scene, caption, and audio variety is a **distribution requirement**, not a
creative preference. It's also why Facebook Fast Track explicitly permitting
AI content matters so much — the platforms' objection is to slop, not to the
tool.

### Shadowban symptoms and response

Watch for: views collapsing to a fraction of normal across *all* recent posts
simultaneously; your posts not appearing under hashtags you used; a sudden
drop in non-follower reach in analytics.

If it happens: **stop posting for 48 hours**, delete nothing (deletion is
read as guilt and doesn't restore reach), audit the last 10 posts against the
hard-nevers, then resume at half cadence with the most clearly-original
content you have. Most suppression is temporary and algorithmic, not a manual
strike.

### The hard-nevers, restated

No children in frame · no tobacco · no realistic weapons · Rufus never
harmed, trapped, or distressed (animal-cruelty classifiers cannot tell satire
from abuse and a false positive is a strike) · no legible third-party
trademarks · no licensed music baked into files · no engagement bait.

## 7 · Time to $1,000/month

**From zero, at 8–12 hours/week.** These are modelled ranges built on the
sourced rates above and typical creator growth curves — not guarantees.
Content quality and one or two lucky breakouts move these numbers more than
any tactic in this document.

### The realistic curve

| Phase | Months | What's happening | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold start** | 1–3 | Daily posting, no traction, learning what lands. Most accounts die here. | **$0** |
| **First traction** | 3–6 | A post breaks out. 10K–30K on TikTok. Creator Rewards unlocks. First brand DMs. | **$50–400/mo** |
| **Compounding** | 6–12 | 50K–150K TikTok. Merch launches. Brand deals become routine. **Fast Track becomes reachable.** | **$400–1,500/mo** |
| **Business** | 12–18 | 100K+ across platforms. Fast Track active. Multiple income lines. | **$1,500–5,000/mo** |

**Most likely time to a $1,000 month: 8–14 months.** With a genuine viral
breakout, as fast as 4–6 months. Without one, 18+ months — or never, which
is the honest outcome for most accounts.

### The three paths, compared

1. **Facebook Fast Track** — cleanest and largest single lever. Needs ~100K
   elsewhere first (or ~20K with exceptional engagement). Guaranteed money
   once in, but time-limited to 3 months, so treat it as a capital injection
   that funds the durable lines rather than as the destination.
2. **Brand deals** — fastest to first dollar. At 20–30K followers you can
   charge $200–400/post; four posts is $1,000. Requires outreach work that
   cannot be automated: a media kit, a rate card, and actual emails.
3. **Merch** — slowest to start, best long-term. 100 units/month at $10
   margin. Realistically needs 50K+ followers before conversion supports
   that, but it's the only line that becomes an actual asset.

**The compound answer: run all three.** They share the same input (audience)
and the same pipeline (already built). Nothing about pursuing Fast Track
prevents brand deals or merch.

## 10 · Barriers to entry and competition

**What protects you (the moat, honestly assessed):**

- **The pipeline is a real advantage.** Canon locks, automated design gates,
  a caption bank, and approval workflow mean you ship consistent quality at a
  cadence a solo human can't match by hand. Most AI-content accounts are
  visibly inconsistent — the character drifts between posts. Yours doesn't.
- **Character equity compounds.** Rufus and BIG SIPPY accumulate recognition
  value. A competitor copying the format still starts at zero recognition.
- **Legal hygiene is a moat at scale.** Trademark clearance, no third-party
  marks, FTC discipline. Brands do due diligence; most AI accounts fail it.

**What does not protect you:**

- **The format is trivially copyable.** Photoreal animal + human situation is
  a prompt, not a secret. Assume imitators within weeks of any breakout.
- **You don't own distribution.** Every follower is rented from a platform
  that can change the rules overnight.
- **AI generation is commoditizing fast.** Today's technical edge is a
  temporary advantage, not a durable one.

**The competition:** a crowded and rapidly saturating field of AI animal
accounts. The reference accounts you studied (photoreal pigeon at 139K likes
/ 112K shares, the horse producer) prove the ceiling is high — and that
they're already there. **Audience fatigue with AI slop is the real threat**,
and the defense is the one thing most competitors skip: a *character* with
consistency and lore, rather than an endless stream of disconnected AI
novelty.

**Your genuine differentiator is the two-mode arc.** Almost nobody in this
category is running actual serialized character development. That's why the
episode and the lore ladder matter beyond their monetization function.

**1. You have no owned audience.** Every follower is rented. A single ban
ends the business overnight. **Fix now, while it's cheap:** a link-in-bio
landing page with an email capture ("Rufus HR memos"). Even 500 emails is an
asset no platform can delete. This is the single biggest structural gap.

**2. Reserve the handles today.** All four platforms plus the .com, right
now, before you post anything. Handle squatting on a successful character is
real, and recovering a handle is slow-to-impossible. Cost: a few dollars.

**3. BIG SIPPY trademark is unfiled while it accrues value.** You did
clearance and confirmed nothing blocks it. But an unregistered mark on a
brand that's about to be printed on merch is exposure — and the person most
likely to file it is someone who sees your account succeed. $250–350/class.

**4. No analytics feedback loop.** The pipeline generates and publishes but
nothing feeds performance back into the format mix. The weekly review
routine exists — **use it to change the calendar**, or the 40/25/15/10/5/5
split is guesswork forever.

**5. Comment moderation at scale.** Animal accounts attract brigading, slurs,
and bot spam. At 100K you cannot read every comment. Set up keyword filters
and hidden-word lists on all four platforms *before* you need them.

**6. Burnout is the most common cause of death.** Daily cadence for 12+
months with no traction for the first three is genuinely hard. The pipeline
mitigates it — but approvals still need a human every day. **Build a two-week
buffer of approved posts** so a sick week doesn't break the streak.

**7. Single point of failure on the Meta connection.** Instagram and Facebook
both ride one OAuth. If that Page is disabled, two of your four platforms go
dark simultaneously — including the one paying you the most.

**8. Merch operations are a real business you haven't scoped.** Sales tax
nexus, returns, customer service, fulfilment delays. Print-on-demand
minimizes it but doesn't eliminate it. Budget hours, not just margin.

**9. FTC disclosure on the very first brand deal.** #ad in the caption, not
buried in hashtags, not "thanks to our friends at." ~$50,120 per violation.
This is in the legal playbook but it becomes live the moment money changes
hands.

**10. YouTube's "Made for Kids" classification.** Flagged above but repeated
here because it's silent and expensive: animal content commonly trips it,
and it disables personalized ads, comments, and notifications. Mark **Not
Made for Kids** deliberately.

**11. You have no content-performance kill criteria.** Decide *now* what
failure looks like — e.g. "if TikTok is under 2,000 followers at month 4,
change the format or stop." Without a pre-committed threshold, sunk-cost
reasoning keeps unprofitable projects alive for years.

**12. The engagement-bait trap.** As growth stalls (and it will), the
temptation is "comment X if…" and follow-for-follow. Every platform demotes
it, and it's corrosive to Rufus's deadpan voice. The character's value is
that it never breaks — including to beg.
