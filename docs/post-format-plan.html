What a Rufus Post Actually Is

Six post formats, what each is for, and how they are assembled — built around one constraint the character imposes and one the platforms do.

The two constraints that decide everything

From the platforms: stills do not travel. Both feeds route reach through video, and a static image posted alone is the weakest thing you can publish. Every format below therefore has a video path.

From the character: Rufus is faceless and voiceless. But voiceless does not mean silent — and that distinction is the single biggest unlock in this plan. See § 03.

01

The hook is the first frame, not the first line

This genre's hook is a specific cognitive event: "wait — is that real?" It fires in well under a second, from the image alone, and everything else in the post is downstream of it.

  • Put the impossibility in frame one. Upright stance plus paws on a human object. Never build to it, never open on an establishing shot — the viewer has already scrolled.
  • The eye contact is the punchline, so it can be delayed. That is the only permissible structure: he is doing the thing, then he looks at you. Two beats, no more.
  • Photoreal is the whole asset. The moment it looks rendered, the hook dies and the post becomes ordinary AI content. Reject any generation that looks like a good photograph or a clean render — see the bible's camera blocks.
  • No text overlay on the image. Burned-in captions signal "content" and break the found-photo illusion. Platform-native caption text only.
02

Six formats

A
The Living Still

A finished still, animated just enough to qualify as video — breathing fur, a blink, a flicker in the strip light, a hair of camera drift. Three to five seconds, seamless loop. Nothing "happens".

Why it matters most: it is the cheapest possible conversion of an image into a distributable video, and the deadpan stillness is on-character rather than a compromise. This should be your highest-volume format.

Build image → videoLength 3–5s loopShare of feed ~40%

Tested, and it works with one fix. A five-second generation from a Rogue still held everything that matters — position, paws, eye contact, the crushed pouch, the notes in the dispenser, and full photorealism — at about 1.2% mean change between frames, which is exactly the intended register. But the camera drifted: roughly 6% difference between the first and last frame, enough that a hard loop visibly jumps. Asking for less movement does not reliably fix it.

Ping-pong the clip instead. Play it forward then reversed, minus the duplicated end frames, and any drift loops perfectly — the measured seam falls from about 6% to under 0.5%. For a clip whose entire premise is that nothing happens, the reversal is invisible. Note that it doubles the runtime, so generate three-second sources to land on a six-second loop.

B
The Turn

He is mid-act — paws in the machine, keypad, fridge — and then turns his head to the lens and holds. The reveal is the beat, and the hold after it is what makes people rewatch.

Five to eight seconds. The hold should feel two beats too long; that discomfort is the joke.

Build image → videoLength 5–8sShare of feed ~25%
C
The Two-Mode Carousel

Slide one: the community man, daylight, helping badly, unopened pouch in shot. Slide two: same raccoon, night, crushed pouch, mid-crime. The swipe is the punchline.

Instagram only — carousels are native there and swipes count as dwell, which is the metric that moves distribution. On TikTok, run it as a two-shot cut instead.

Build 2 stillsPlatform IG carouselShare of feed ~15%
D
The CCTV Drop

Black-and-white, high corner angle, compression artefacts, night-vision blowout. Framed as recovered evidence rather than a photograph.

Its job is register variety. A feed of identical candid phone snaps reads as a formula — which is the specific behaviour that degrades recommendation. This is your variance, and it doubles as proof for the running "it wasn't Rufus" denial.

Build still or videoMode Rogue onlyShare of feed ~10%
E
The Aftermath

He is not in frame. An emptied shelf, a door ajar, one crushed pouch on the floor, a single paw print. The audience supplies him.

Rare — perhaps one in twenty. It only works once the character is established, and it is the format that proves the audience actually knows who he is.

Build still → living stillMode Rogue onlyShare of feed ~5%
F
The Tentpole

The pivot, the promotion, the incident. Higher production, longer, posted deliberately rather than on cadence. Around one a month.

Push these as carousels or longer cuts and let the surrounding weeks be setup. The pivot post specifically should carry no caption at all.

Build multi-shotCadence ~monthlyShare of feed ~5%
03

Sound — the unlock

Voiceless does not mean silent

TikTok is a sound-on culture and a silent video reads as lifeless there. The character forbids narration — but it does not forbid diegetic sound: the sound the scene itself would make. Street hum and a distant siren. Fluorescent buzz. The ATM's key beeps. A fridge compressor. And above all, the straw — the wet pull, and the dry rattle when the pouch is empty.

That last one is doing real work. A slurp is inherently funny, it is the character's signature act, and it is audio branding you own — the equivalent of the crushed pouch, for ears. Recognisable in one second with the screen face-down.

Generate it natively — the audio comes with the video

The standalone audio tool is text-to-speech only, which is easy to mistake for "no sound generation available." It is not. A dozen video models generate synchronized audio as part of the render, from the same start image, in one step — so ambience is a generation parameter, not a sourcing problem.

Tested and confirmed. A five-second image-to-video render with audio enabled returned a real AAC track at about −30 dBFS — quiet, correct for a bed. Its spectral profile ran 55–69% low-frequency, 28–32% mid, under 15% high, smooth and transient-free across the whole clip, which is the signature of continuous traffic rumble and electrical hum. Speech would have spiked the mid band and produced sharp onsets; music would have shown structure and stronger highs. Neither appeared. The negative instruction held.

Two things to get right

Negative-prompt the audio explicitly. These models will happily add speech, music and footsteps. Every prompt needs the audio equivalent of a negative block — no music, no voices, no narration, no dialogue — or the voiceless rule breaks on the first render.

Do not ping-pong a clip with audio. Reversing the video reverses the track. Pure ambience mostly survives it, but any transient plays backwards and reads as wrong. Loop the picture and lay a forward audio pass across the doubled length instead.

Still record the straw yourself

Generated ambience solves the bed. It will not reliably give you the signature — the wet pull and the dry rattle of an empty pouch. A phone and a real foil pouch gets that in five minutes, you own it outright, and it becomes a protectable audio mark rather than a model output. Generate the room; record the character.

  • Design for sound-off first, then add the bed. Most Instagram viewing is muted; the post must be complete in silence and better with sound.
  • No trending audio. Meta's label deals cover personal and creator use, not commercial — a monetised business profile using the trending catalogue has a licensing gap independent of everything else. Ambient beds and original sound only.
  • The dry rattle is the Rogue signature. Empty pouch, straw scraping air. Use it to close Rogue posts the way the caption sign-off closes them in text.

Do not export square

1:1 was tested and rejected. Rufus is a vertical subject and the crushed pouch — the mode indicator — sits at his feet, so a square crop clips the one object that tells a viewer which Rufus they are looking at. Facebook accepts 4:5 in feed, so square buys nothing and costs the signal. Cut Reels and Feed only.

Crop bias, not centre-crop

A blind centre crop puts him too close to the frame edge and drops the machine. Bias the horizontal window slightly right of centre so the cash machine survives as context on the left, and bias the vertical window down so the pouch stays in shot. The compositions are built with this headroom, but the bias has to be set per shot — check the first frame before batching the rest.

05

The production run

Batch weekly, publish daily. Everything below is one sitting.

01
Pick the week's acts
Seven scenes from the episode list, alternating modes — roughly four Rogue to three community man, so the arc keeps moving without the wholesome half disappearing.
02
Generate stills in one batch
Fixed subject block, mode-appropriate camera block, no anatomical marker instruction. Over-generate — expect to bin a third for scale drift, over-polish, or a band that renders at an angle.
03
One object-level edit pass
Add or crush the pouch, repair defects. Never a second pass — realism degrades faster than the fix is worth.
04
Composite the wordmark
Run the band script on any frame where the pouch lettering is legible enough to matter. Skip it where the pouch is small or crushed.
05
Animate
Each approved still becomes a Living Still or a Turn. This is where the week's volume comes from — one still yields one video and one carousel slide.
06
Sound pass
Ambient bed per scene, plus the straw on any post where the pouch is in use. Silent-first check before the bed goes on.
07
Score before you queue
Run the finished cuts through a virality/retention check and let it re-order the week — publish the strongest on the days you have the most audience, not in the order you made them.
08
Caption, label, log
Mode-correct formula and sign-off. AI label at upload. Log each asset's scene, mode, prompt version and rights basis — the source log, adapted.
On automation

The pipeline above is deliberately batch-then-schedule, not generate-on-trigger. A daily automated generation would publish unreviewed frames, and roughly a third of generations fail quality — human hands, scale drift, an angled band. The step that must stay manual is selection. Automate the scheduling and the publishing; never automate the accept/reject.

06

What to measure

The reference accounts in this genre run enormous reach and almost no community — share-to-like around 0.6, comment-to-like around 0.002. That is the trap this whole design is built to avoid, so measure the thing you are trying to fix.

If comment rate stays flat at the genre baseline after a month, the fix is not more posts — it is a harder ask in the caption, or more ambiguous frames where the audience has to argue about which Rufus they are looking at.
