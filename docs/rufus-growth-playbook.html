RUFUS · GROWTH & POSTING PLAYBOOK · OPERATING MANUAL

Two posts a day, one raccoon, zero narration

How Rufus the Raccoon and Rufus Gone Rogue get produced, approved, and published daily on Instagram and TikTok — and the exposure strategy the cadence serves. Companion to the character bible, the post-format plan, and the caption bank.

1 · One account, not two

Run ONE brand account per platform, same handle on both. The community-man → rogue turn is not a theme, it is the content engine: the two-mode carousel, the pivot arc, and the "which Rufus today?" open are all jokes that require both moods in one feed. Splitting the modes into two accounts deletes the punchline from both — the community account becomes a mild animal page, the rogue account becomes context-free chaos, and neither converts a viewer into a follower the way the contrast does.

The practical costs of splitting are just as real: two accounts halve posting velocity per account (velocity is the primary early-growth input), split social proof at the stage where follower count itself drives follows, and double the moderation, approval, and flag-risk surface.

Revisit at ~100K+ — and the correct spinoff then is a BIG SIPPY product-brand page (merch engine, in-world "beverage company" posting), not a second Rufus. The character never splits.

Format mix follows the post-format plan: Living Still ~40% · The Turn ~25% · Two-Mode Carousel ~15% · CCTV Drop ~10% · Aftermath ~5% · Tentpole ~5%. The rotation calendar enforces the mix and the community/rogue balance (roughly 60/40 community-forward early — the rogue hits harder when the baseline is established).

  • Timing: evening US windows (6–10pm ET) for the video; midday for the still. Consistency beats optimal timing for the first 90 days — the algorithm learns the schedule before the audience does.
  • Ceiling: never more than 4 posts/day per platform. Past that, identical-looking AI content starts pattern-matching to spam farming, which is the actual distribution killer (not the AI label).
  • Never skip a day. A missed day costs more than a mediocre post. The bank of gated, approved-but-unposted assets is the buffer — the approval routine holds unposted approvals for exactly this.

3 · Audience and retention mechanics

Target: 16–34, the animal-meme / absurdist-humor overlap — the audience that made the reference accounts (photoreal pigeon, horse producer) run six-figure share counts. Shares are the metric that matters: this content is a social object ("look at this") more than a watch object.

  • Lore is the retention engine. The mode arc gives a casual viewer a reason to visit the profile ("wait, is this the same raccoon from the ATM?"), and profile visits convert to follows at several times the feed rate. Pinned posts: the pivot image, the best Turn video, and one carousel — the three-post lore starter kit.
  • Comments are content. Reply in character or not at all: Rufus never explains, never apologizes, never breaks deadpan. The account's *own* comment under a chaotic post ("he is doing his best") reliably becomes the top comment and doubles comment velocity.
  • Comment-bait is built into the captions — the 70/30 bank exists because deadpan understatement ("new hire") makes viewers supply the outrage themselves, and an unrelated biology fact under a crime scene makes them tag friends to share the confusion.

4 · Captions, hashtags, sound

  • Captions come from the caption bank, 70% in-scene irony / 30% unrelated raccoon biology, rotation tracked in the post log so the split holds over time. Max 2 sentences (biology exempt). Never explain the joke, never use exclamation marks, never address the camera as the account owner.
  • Hashtags: 3–5 niche tags on IG (#raccoonsofinstagram tier, not #funny tier), 3–4 on TikTok. Broad tags put the content in fast-scroll contexts where completion rate dies.
  • Music on stills and carousels — platform pickers only. At post time, add audio through IG's music sticker limited to the Meta Sound Collection (business accounts are restricted from the trending catalog — commercial-use gap established in the legal playbook) and TikTok photo-mode audio from the commercial library. A trending-audio still on TikTok is the single cheapest reach multiplier available to a static image.
  • Never bake licensed music into files (the Gunshotta rule: sync + master licenses or nothing). Videos ship with their native generated ambience — the store hum and street rumble are ownable audio branding, and platform-added music can sit on top when the trend fits.
  • Video durations: 6–10s loopable is the workhorse (the 8s wrecked-store clip is the template — completion % is the ranked metric and a clean loop double-counts). Living Stills 5–7s. Tentpoles 15–20s. One 60s+ episode per week exists for TikTok's 1min+ originality bar — generated fresh each Sunday, never cut from the week's posts (see §5b), with the week's shorts harvested from it.

5 · What gets flagged, what gets demonetized

Hard nevers — enforced by canon and by the gates

• Children in frame (adults-only rule; also killed a generation job outright)
• Tobacco imagery (standing instruction; ad-exclusion + age-gate risk)
• Realistic weapons in Rufus's possession
• Rufus appearing harmed, trapped, or in distress — animal-cruelty classifiers cannot tell satire from abuse, and a false positive is a strike
• Legible third-party marks — the Yankees-cap lesson; run delogo.py + the per-frame check on every asset
• Baked-in commercial music — instant Content ID mute/strike

  • Label the content as AI on Meta. Required for photorealistic AI media, and the earlier research holds: the label is not a ranking penalty — mass-produced repetition is. Scene, caption, and audio variety is therefore a distribution requirement, not a creative preference. The rotation calendar is the enforcement.
  • Aggregator/originality rules cut the other way here: everything is original, first-party content — the April 2026 IG aggregator policy that threatens repost accounts is a tailwind for this one.
  • Monetization reality check: platform payouts (TikTok Creativity Program, IG bonuses) are pocket change and gated on 1min+ originals or invitations. The business is BIG SIPPY merch and brand deals — which is exactly why the trademark hygiene and the FTC disclosure rules from the legal playbook stay load-bearing. Any paid post gets #ad in the caption, no exceptions.

5b · The weekly 60-second episode

How it gets made. No generation model exceeds 20 seconds, so the episode is necessarily an assembly: ~7 frame-chained clips (flux_3_video, 8–10s, native ambience), where the last frame of clip N becomes the start frame of clip N+1 alongside the canon reference — that chain is what holds identity across cuts. Claude writes the 7-beat "Turn, extended" beat sheet, gates each clip, assembles (tools/assemble-episode.py: xfaded seams, one continuous ambience bed, loudnorm, beats.json sidecar), and harvests (tools/cut-shorts.py). Higgsfield renders pixels; everything editorial is Claude's.

Generated fresh, always. A compilation of already-posted clips is duplicate content, and TikTok's payout program is gated on original 1min+ video — assembling the episode from the week's posts would forfeit the one thing the episode exists for. The calendar therefore runs episode-first: Sunday generates the episode, and the week's shorts are harvested from it.

Where 60s is actually required: TikTok only. Creator Rewards pays RPM on 1min+ originals (plus follower/view thresholds — re-check terms before relying on numbers). Instagram has no duration minimum at all, and the long cut actively loses there: Reels ranks on completion rate, where a 10s loop beats a 60s episode every time. IG and Facebook get the shorts, never the episode.

Harvesting is the economics. Seven fresh clips replace roughly two days of separate daily generation, so the episode pays for itself. The beats.json sidecar makes cutting mechanical; each short is re-captioned from the bank so it stands alone; the episode log guarantees no beat posts twice. The episode also gives the lore its spine — daily posts are disconnected gags, and lore is what converts viewers into followers.

Expansion. Facebook Reels is wired now — same Meta connection as IG, zero new asset work, and an older-skewing audience where animal content still travels. YouTube Shorts is next: same 9:16 asset, real revenue share, 60s fits natively. X is skipped for video — weak non-news video reach, Premium-gated monetization, no cold-start discovery — though Rufus's deadpan caption voice would suit a future text-only X account as a separate play.

7 · A dedicated repo: yes

Move Rufus to its own repo (rufus-media). Routines run in fresh sessions that clone the repo on every firing; the design sandbox is heavy with unrelated work and getting heavier with every committed asset. The dedicated repo gives fast clones, a Rufus-specific CLAUDE.md (canon, gate usage, posting rules — so every routine session starts already knowing the rules), a clean automation scope, and a commit history that doubles as the content archive.
